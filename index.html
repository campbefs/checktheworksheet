How should child care be split?

 

The Worksheet charges the higher earner 87.7% ($4,559/yr) of the child care, while he holds 64% of the money after the order on paper and 53% of it after tax.

The MA credit for children under 13 is fixed at zero throughout.

Assumptions and method

Box 2 puts the children primarily with the lower earner, who receives, while the higher earner pays. Child care above is a combined weekly total per parent, capped at $430 per child; switching the Child care section on already reflects it in the order and in the after-tax figures above. The MA Child and Family Tax Credit for children under 13 is fixed at zero throughout, the same convention as every income-range chart on this site. This calculator models Massachusetts only; no other state's worksheet is implemented here. Method: model/worksheet.py and model/net_position.py, ported and tested in assets/js/lib/worksheet.js and assets/js/calculator.test.js. The Child care section's three rules all come from model/childcare_post_transfer.py: "as the Worksheet does it" is today's Line 6b; "on income after the order" allocates on each parent's resulting share of income once the base order is set, instead of their share beforehand; "on money after tax" allocates on each parent's share of the money after tax and FICA at the no-child-care order, leaving out the refundable credits CJ-D 304 has no field for, and moves the order the same way "on income after the order" does.
